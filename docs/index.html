Computer Vision project

PillScope

OpenCV · no machine learning

Give it a photo of a pill. It finds the pill, measures its shape and colour, and matches its speckle pattern against a database of 251 pills. If the pill is not in the database, it says so.

How it works

Each of the eight Computer Vision labs became one part of the pipeline. The labs that are not part of the identification are still in the app, in the Explore tab.

  1. Find the pill
    Lab 7 · segmentation

    Compare every pixel with the background colour, let Otsu's method pick the cut-off, and keep the largest blob. Region growing from the corners is the alternative.

  2. Measure shape and colour
    Lab 4 · colour spaces

    Circularity and aspect ratio name the shape. The base colour is measured in HSV and LAB, and strongly coloured speckles are reported separately.

  3. Clean up and find edges
    Labs 1 and 6

    Crop to the pill, convert to grey, median blur to remove noise, CLAHE to lift contrast, then Canny edges.

  4. Describe the pill
    Labs 4, 5, 8

    A colour histogram, a rotation-proof FFT texture profile, HOG edge directions, and SIFT keypoints.

  5. Match and decide
    Lab 8 · SIFT

    SIFT keypoints are paired with Lowe's ratio test. Only pairs that agree on one rotation, scale and shift are counted. Right matches have 29 or more; pills that are not in the database have about 3.

How well it works

Top-1 accuracy: the first answer is the right pill. Tested against the 251 pills in the database.

What these numbers do and do not show

The tests check whether a pill the database already knows is recognised again after rotation, shrinking, noise or dimming. They do not show that the app can identify a pill it has never seen, because the dataset has photos only, with no imprint, dose or maker information.

The dataset's rotated files are often numbered differently from the photo they came from (only 75 of 227 match). The right answer was found by comparing pixels, not by trusting the file names.

Limits

  • Speckle pattern only. It suits pills that look different from each other. Pills that differ only by a printed imprint would need text recognition, which is not included.
  • One pill per photo, on a plain, light background.
  • Samples on this page are saved results. To run it on your own photo, get the code from GitHub and start python run.py.
